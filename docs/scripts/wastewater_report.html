getting the amount of each soil suitability level for each regional planning commission

import numpy as np


info_soil_data = [info_ACRPC, info_CCRPC, info_CVRPC, info_LCPC, info_MARC, info_NWRPC, info_BCRC]
whole_vermont_info = pd.concat(info_soil_data, ignore_index=True)
info_soil_data.append(whole_vermont_info)
info_soil_data_name = ["ACRPC", "CCRPC", "CVRPC", "LCPC", "MARC", "NWRPC", "BCRC", "Vermont"]

for i in range(len(info_soil_data)):
    # print(info_soil_data[i].sort_values(by='Suitability').groupby(['Suitability'])['Acres'].sum())
    # print(np.sort(info_soil_data[i]['Suitability'].unique()))
    plt.bar(height = info_soil_data[i].sort_values(by='Suitability').groupby(['Suitability'])['Acres'].sum(), x = np.sort(info_soil_data[i]['Suitability'].unique()))
    plt.xticks(rotation = 45)
    plt.title(f"Soil Suitability Distribution by Acres for {info_soil_data_name[i]}")
    plt.xlabel("Soil Suitability Level")
    plt.ylabel("Number of Acres")
    plt.show()

whole_vermont_info = pd.concat(info_soil_data, ignore_index=True)

What these charts are going over is the number of acres of each level of soil suitability for septic tanks for each regional planning commission, as well as Vermont as a whole. There is an important caveat that the Vermont section is not all of Vermont, but rather all of the data we have, as there are a few regional planning commissions who’s data we don’t have.

What these charts show is that there is a lot of variability between regions in terms of the distribution of soil suitability. By this I mean that some regional planning commissions have radically different soil suitabilities, with one example being that the NWRCP has ‘not suited’ as by far its largest soil suitability level, while for BCRC ‘moderately suited’ is its largest soil suitability level, with ‘not suited’ coming in a distant second.

Looking a Vermont as a whole, either ‘not suited’ or ‘moderately suited’ is the largest soil suitability level for most regions, though CVRCP bucks that trend with ‘marginally suited’ being its most common soil suitability level. This frequency is shown on the Vermont total numbers, with ‘not suited’ being the most common soil suitability level, ‘moderately suited’ being the next most common, and ‘marginally suited’ being a close third. While the top 3 are all pretty close in terms of acres, the other two, ‘not rated’ and ‘well suited’, have a lot fewer acres. What this means for Vermont as a whole is a lot of its land is not really suited for septic tanks between all the acres that are ‘not suited’ or ‘marginally suited’. After that a good amount of land is ‘moderately suitable’, indicating that septic tanks could be placed there, though conditions are not perfect for it. And lastly, there is a relatively small percentage of land that is ‘well suited’, and no regions have ‘well suited’ as their top two most common soil suitability levels, indicating that no region of Vermont is perfect for septic systems.
